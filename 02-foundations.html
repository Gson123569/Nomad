Wealth Tier 2 of 8: Foundations

Foundations Tier — $5M-$20M

Building the structural bedrock: when family considerations and multi-entity planning enter the wealth preservation equation.

The Snapshot

What defines this tier in 30 seconds

Serial entrepreneur with $12M after exit, married with one child. Has optimized personal tax through Tier 1 strategies but now faces complexity: spouse's career, child's education, aging parents, and the realization that simple residency arbitrage is insufficient for multi-generational wealth.

  • To: Family wealth architecture

The Architecture

What changes at this level and why it matters

What Changes at This Level

At $5M-$20M, you cross the threshold where individual tax optimization becomes insufficient. Your wealth now extends beyond your lifetime, implicating your spouse, children, and potentially grandchildren. The questions shift from "How do I pay less tax this year?" to "How do I preserve this wealth for three generations?"

The structural shift is from personal to familial. You begin establishing holding companies to centralize investment ownership. You explore trust structures—perhaps initially simple, but laying groundwork for more sophisticated arrangements. You confront the reality that your children will inherit not just wealth but complexity, and you must prepare them for it.

This tier also introduces the concept of "substance"—the requirement that your structures have genuine economic purpose beyond tax reduction. Tax authorities worldwide have tightened rules around beneficial ownership, economic activity, and beneficial substance. Your structures must now pass scrutiny that Tier 1 personal arrangements rarely faced.

Key Concepts You Must Understand

Non-Dom Status
Non-domiciled status allows individuals to be tax residents without being domiciled, often enabling taxation on a remittance basis (only income brought into the country) rather than worldwide income.
Why it matters: The UK's non-dom regime (though recently modified) and similar structures in Ireland, Malta, and Cyprus can provide decades of tax efficiency for foreign-sourced wealth.
Holding Company
A holding company is a parent entity that owns shares in operating subsidiaries, enabling centralized control, tax-efficient profit distribution, and simplified ownership transfers.
Why it matters: Properly structured holding companies can eliminate withholding taxes on dividends, enable tax-free asset transfers, and provide liability protection.
Substance Requirements
Substance requirements mandate that companies claiming tax benefits must have genuine economic activity—real offices, employees, board meetings, and decision-making in that jurisdiction.
Why it matters: Post-BEPS, tax authorities aggressively challenge structures without substance. Paper companies face denial of treaty benefits and potential penalties.
Discretionary Trust
A discretionary trust gives the trustee discretion over when and how much to distribute to beneficiaries, providing flexibility and asset protection but requiring careful tax planning.
Why it matters: Discretionary trusts can protect assets from creditors, divorcing spouses, and spendthrift beneficiaries while enabling tax-efficient wealth transfer.
Participation Exemption
Participation exemptions allow holding companies to receive dividends and capital gains from subsidiaries without taxation, eliminating multiple layers of corporate tax.
Why it matters: Properly structured, a Luxembourg or Dutch holding company can receive global dividends with zero withholding tax and zero corporate tax.
Banking Diversification
Banking diversification means maintaining accounts across multiple jurisdictions and institutions to reduce counterparty risk and increase financial privacy.
Why it matters: Single-jurisdiction banking creates vulnerability to asset freezes, currency controls, and institutional failure. Diversification is essential risk management.

The Playbook

Strategy 01

Non-Dom Regime Optimization

+

The Concept

Non-domiciled status is one of the most powerful tools in international tax planning. While the UK's regime has been modified (and will be abolished from 2025), Ireland, Malta, and Cyprus continue to offer attractive non-dom programs. The core benefit is remittance-basis taxation: you pay tax only on income brought into the country, not on worldwide earnings.

The Execution

  • Establish residence in a non-dom jurisdiction—Ireland requires 183+ days annually or 280 days over two years
  • Demonstrate that your domicile of origin (typically your father's domicile at your birth) differs from your residence
  • Structure income sources to maximize remittance-basis benefits—foreign investment income and capital gains are most advantageous
  • Maintain clean capital accounts: segregate pre-residence wealth from post-residence earnings
  • Pay the annual remittance basis charge (in UK: £30,000-£60,000 for long-term residents)
  • Work with tax advisors to ensure proper documentation of domicile and remittance tracking
Risk Warning: Non-dom regimes are politically vulnerable. The UK's abolition demonstrates how quickly these benefits can disappear. Always have contingency plans and avoid making irreversible commitments based on non-dom status alone.

Case Study: The O'Brien Family

James O'Brien, with $15M from a fintech exit and Irish heritage, established Irish residence under the non-dom regime. By segregating his pre-residence wealth and remitting only necessary living expenses, he pays Irish tax on approximately €150,000 annually while his global investment portfolio of $12M grows largely outside the Irish tax net. His annual remittance basis charge is €200,000—expensive, but a fraction of what full worldwide taxation would cost.

Strategy 02

European Holding Company Structure

+

The Concept

A properly structured European holding company can serve as the central node of your investment architecture, receiving dividends from operating subsidiaries and portfolio investments with minimal or zero taxation. Luxembourg SOPARFI, Dutch BV/CV structures, and Irish holding companies each offer distinct advantages depending on your asset mix and distribution needs.

The Execution

  • Select jurisdiction based on your asset types: Luxembourg for real estate and IP, Netherlands for operational subsidiaries, Ireland for portfolio investments
  • Establish substance: real office, local directors, board meetings in jurisdiction, and economic decision-making
  • Structure to meet participation exemption requirements—typically 10%+ ownership for 12+ months
  • Leverage the EU Parent-Subsidiary Directive to eliminate withholding taxes on intra-EU dividends
  • Consider the Dutch "innovation box" for IP-intensive businesses—effective 7% tax rate
  • Maintain proper documentation: shareholder registers, board minutes, transfer pricing documentation
Risk Warning: Holding companies without genuine substance are prime targets for tax authority challenge. The cost of maintaining proper substance (office, directors, compliance) can exceed €100,000 annually. Ensure the tax savings justify the structural costs.

Case Study: Laurent Holdings S.a.r.l.

Marie Laurent, with $18M from a SaaS exit, established a Luxembourg SOPARFI holding her shares in three operating companies across Europe. The structure receives approximately €800,000 annually in dividends from subsidiaries. Under Luxembourg's participation exemption, these dividends are not subject to corporate tax. Marie draws a modest salary and dividends personally, keeping her effective tax rate under 15% while maintaining full EU market access and credibility.

Strategy 03

Double Tax Treaty Network Optimization

+

The Concept

Double Tax Treaties (DTTs) form a complex web of bilateral agreements that determine taxing rights. At the Foundations tier, you begin actively mapping and optimizing your position within this network—structuring investments through treaty-favorable jurisdictions, obtaining Certificates of Residence, and ensuring you can claim maximum treaty benefits.

The Execution

  • Map the complete treaty network between your income sources, investment holdings, and residence jurisdictions
  • Identify the most favorable routes for each income type—dividends, interest, royalties, capital gains
  • Understand Limitation on Benefits (LOB) clauses and Principal Purpose Test (PPT) provisions
  • Obtain Certificates of Residence from your tax authority to support treaty claims
  • Structure investments through jurisdictions with favorable treaty networks—Netherlands (extensive), Luxembourg, Singapore
  • Document the commercial rationale for your structure beyond tax minimization
Risk Warning: Treaty shopping is increasingly challenged. The OECD's PPT allows denial of treaty benefits if obtaining those benefits was a principal purpose of an arrangement. Always ensure genuine commercial substance and document non-tax business purposes.

Case Study: The Fischer Investment Structure

Thomas Fischer, German resident with $14M, held US equities directly, paying 30% US withholding tax on dividends. By restructuring through a Dutch CV holding company, he leveraged the US-Netherlands treaty to reduce withholding to 15%, saving approximately €45,000 annually. The structure cost €25,000 to establish and €15,000 annually to maintain—paying for itself in year one while providing additional asset protection benefits.

Strategy 04

Substance Compliance Framework

+

The Concept

Post-BEPS (Base Erosion and Profit Shifting), tax authorities worldwide require that companies claiming tax benefits have genuine economic substance in that jurisdiction. This means real offices, local directors who make actual decisions, board meetings held in-country, and employees commensurate with the company's activities. Substance is no longer optional—it's a prerequisite for defensible tax planning.

The Execution

  • Establish physical presence: leased office space appropriate to your business scale (not just virtual office)
  • Appoint qualified local directors with actual decision-making authority, not mere nominees
  • Hold board meetings in the jurisdiction with proper minutes documenting strategic decisions
  • Maintain employees or contractors performing genuine functions—accounting, administration, management
  • Keep corporate records, books, and banking in the jurisdiction
  • Prepare transfer pricing documentation demonstrating arm's-length transactions with related parties
Risk Warning: Inadequate substance can result in denial of treaty benefits, reassessment of tax liabilities with penalties, and potential criminal charges for tax evasion in egregious cases. The cost of proper substance is high but the cost of inadequate substance is far higher.

Case Study: Substance Transformation

Alexandra's BVI holding company, which owned European real estate, was challenged by German tax authorities who denied treaty benefits due to lack of substance. She restructured by establishing a Luxembourg SOPARFI with a real office in Luxembourg City, two local directors, quarterly board meetings, and a full-time administrator. Annual substance costs increased from €15,000 to €180,000, but the structure now withstands scrutiny and preserves approximately €400,000 annually in treaty benefits.

Strategy 05

Multi-Jurisdictional Banking

+

The Concept

Banking diversification is essential risk management at the Foundations tier. Single-jurisdiction banking creates vulnerability to asset freezes, currency controls, institutional failure, and political interference. A properly diversified banking structure spans multiple jurisdictions, currencies, and institution types—private banks, commercial banks, and potentially neobanks for operational efficiency.

The Execution

  • Maintain primary private banking relationships in 2-3 stable jurisdictions—Switzerland, Singapore, and Luxembourg are traditional choices
  • Establish operational accounts in your residence jurisdiction for daily expenses and local compliance
  • Consider US banking access for USD transactions and US market investments, even for non-residents
  • Maintain accounts in multiple currencies to reduce FX risk and increase flexibility
  • Establish relationships with institutions of different sizes—large global banks for stability, boutique private banks for service
  • Regularly review and refresh banking relationships as regulations and institution health evolve
Risk Warning: Multi-jurisdictional banking increases compliance complexity—each account may trigger reporting obligations (FBAR, FATCA, CRS). Ensure your tax advisors are aware of all accounts and that reporting is complete and accurate.

Case Study: The Chen Banking Matrix

William Chen, with $16M in assets, maintains a four-jurisdiction banking structure: a Swiss private bank for wealth management (CHF 8M), a Singapore commercial bank for Asia-Pacific operations (SGD 3M equivalent), a US brokerage for American investments (USD 4M), and a UK bank for European expenses (GBP 800k). This structure survived the 2023 US regional banking crisis unscathed and provides currency diversification that has saved approximately 3% in FX costs annually.

Strategy 06

Foundational Trust Structures

+

The Concept

Trusts are the foundational building blocks of multi-generational wealth preservation. At this tier, you establish your first trust structures—initially focused on asset protection and succession planning, but laying the groundwork for more sophisticated arrangements as wealth grows. The key jurisdictions are those with strong trust law, political stability, and favorable tax treatment: Cayman Islands, Bermuda, Jersey, Guernsey, and increasingly, Singapore and Dubai.

The Execution

  • Define trust objectives: asset protection, succession planning, tax efficiency, or charitable purposes
  • Select jurisdiction based on your goals—Cayman for flexibility, Bermuda for insurance structures, Channel Islands for UK connections
  • Choose trust type: discretionary for flexibility, interest-in-possession for income rights, purpose trusts for specific objectives
  • Appoint qualified trustees—professional trust companies with strong regulatory standing
  • Consider protector provisions to retain some oversight without undermining trust validity
  • Fund the trust with appropriate assets, understanding potential gift or transfer tax implications
Risk Warning: Trusts are complex legal structures with significant tax implications. Improperly structured trusts can trigger immediate tax liabilities, fail to provide intended asset protection, or be disregarded by courts. Professional legal and tax advice is essential and non-negotiable.

Case Study: The Morrison Family Trust

Robert Morrison, with $19M from a business sale, established a Cayman Islands discretionary trust for his wife and two children, settling $8M of liquid investments. The trust provides asset protection against potential future creditors, enables professional management of family wealth, and creates a framework for educating his children about wealth responsibility. While the trust provides minimal immediate tax benefits (Robert is US tax resident), it establishes the structural foundation for future optimization when his children, UK citizens, become beneficiaries.
